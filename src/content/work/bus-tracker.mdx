## A small question, asked often

“When is the next bus?” is a small question that can lead to a surprising amount of phone checking.

BusTracker explores putting that information on a dedicated round display. The ambition is a useful glance: enough information to orient someone without pulling them into another app.

This sample narrative is prepared for replacement with the actual build story. The device illustration and departure times are fictional references, not a live feed or a product photograph.

## Give one number room to breathe

The next departure is the primary information. Route identity and the following departure provide context. Secondary details should support the glance rather than compete with it.

The visual direction uses scale to separate those roles. It should be evaluated on the physical display, from the distance at which the device is actually used.

## When the connection is imperfect

A connected object has to communicate more than a successful response. A lost connection, an unavailable service and an old departure estimate are different situations.

The portfolio story should show the real implemented states, including what happens when fresh data is unavailable. A polished normal state is only one part of the interaction.

## Does it earn its place?

A focused connected object can be a useful experiment in restraint. The design question is whether it earns a place in a person’s routine through clarity and reliability.

The next layer of evidence is the working object: normal and unavailable-data states, the source of departure information, and what daily use taught the maker. Explain which parts were designed directly, implemented with AI assistance and checked on hardware.
